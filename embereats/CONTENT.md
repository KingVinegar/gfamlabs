# EmberEats - Content & Voice Guide v1.0

## Voice

### North Star
> A seasoned camper who's cooked hundreds of meals outdoors and is quietly helping you make a great one.

Not a chef. Not a blogger. Not a brand. This is your friend's dad who always made the best campfire breakfast and finally wrote it all down in a notebook he's handing you.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What it means | Example |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| **Practical** | Gets to the point. No fluff, no food writing. | "Cut potatoes into 1-inch cubes" not "lovingly dice your potatoes into rustic chunks" |
| **Experienced** | Sounds like someone who's done this | "This works better if you prep at home" not "Consider prepping at home for convenience" |
| **Warm** | Friendly without being peppy | "A good one for the first night" not "PERFECT for your first night!! 🔥" |
| **Honest** | Says when something is tricky | "Takes some practice to get the heat right" not every recipe is "easy and delicious" |

### Voice is NOT

- **A food blogger.** No "This recipe is SO good you guys." No life stories before the recipe.
- **A survival instructor.** No "In a wilderness scenario..." This is car camping, not Bear Grylls.
- **Overly enthusiastic.** No exclamation marks in recipe content. No emoji. No ALL CAPS.
- **Clinical.** No "Ensure proper internal temperature of 165F for all poultry products." Say "Make sure chicken is cooked all the way through."
- **Preachy about nutrition.** No "packed with protein" or "a healthy source of fiber."

## Recipe Content Structure

### Recipe Name
- Short, descriptive, appetizing
- Use common names people actually say at camp
- 2-5 words ideal

**Good:** "Campfire Mac & Cheese", "Foil Packet Fajitas", "One-Pot Chili"
**Bad:** "Smoky Chipotle-Lime Grilled Chicken with Cilantro-Avocado Crema", "Easy Campfire Recipe #7"

### Introduction (1-2 sentences)
Why this recipe works at camp. Practical context, not food writing.

**Good:** "This works on any camp stove and feeds a crowd. Prep the dry mix at home to save time."
**Bad:** "There's nothing quite like the aroma of sizzling onions wafting through the crisp mountain air..."

### Ingredients
- List format, one per line
- Amount first, then item: "2 cups rice", "1 lb ground beef"
- Use common measurements (cups, tablespoons, lbs) not grams/ml
- Include "prep at home" notes inline: "1 onion, diced (prep at home)"
- Round to practical camping quantities. Nobody measures 2/3 cup at a campsite.

### Equipment Needed
- Only list what's specific to this recipe beyond basics
- Assume they have: plates, utensils, a knife, a cutting board
- List non-obvious items: "Dutch oven with lid", "heavy-duty aluminum foil", "cast iron skillet"

### Steps
- Numbered, concise, one action per step when possible
- Start each step with a verb: "Heat", "Add", "Stir", "Cover"
- Include timing cues: "Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally"
- Include sensory cues: "until onions are translucent", "until edges start to brown"
- Keep steps short. If a step needs more than 2 sentences, break it into two steps.

**Good step:** "Heat oil in the skillet over medium heat. Add onions and cook 5 minutes until soft."
**Bad step:** "First, you'll want to take your beautiful cast iron skillet and carefully place it over your camp stove, then wait for it to heat up to a nice medium temperature, at which point you can add a generous glug of olive oil, swirling to coat, and then gently add your diced onions."

### Pro Tip (REQUIRED - every recipe gets one)
- One sentence. Practical camping wisdom.
- The kind of thing you'd pencil in the margin after making this a hundred times.
- This is a KEY DIFFERENTIATOR. These tips are what make EmberEats feel like it was written by a real camper, not scraped from a food blog.

**Two categories of pro tips (use whichever fits best):**

1. **Cooking wisdom** -- Makes this specific recipe work better at camp.
2. **Camp craft** -- Adjacent camping knowledge triggered by the recipe. The kind of thing Grandad would mention offhand while stirring the pot.

**Good cooking wisdom:**
- "Freeze the ground beef before packing. It doubles as an ice pack and thaws by dinner."
- "Pre-mix the dry ingredients at home in a zip-lock. Label it with a Sharpie."
- "Wrap the skillet handle in a bandana. It gets hotter than you think over a campfire."

**Good camp craft (the differentiator):**
- "Toss citrus peels into the fire after eating. The oils help keep mosquitoes back."
- "Save the bacon grease in a tin. It's the best fire starter you'll ever have."
- "Rub the outside of the pot with dish soap before putting it over the fire. The soot wipes right off."
- "If you're cooking with garlic, rub a cut clove on mosquito bites. Takes the itch out."
- "Leftover coffee grounds work as a hand scrub to get the campfire smell off."
- "Eggshells crushed into your camp garden compost or scattered around your cooler area deter slugs."

**Bad (don't do these):**
- "For a vegetarian option, substitute tempeh." (That's a variation, not a tip.)
- "This recipe is great for kids!" (That's marketing, not wisdom.)
- "Always practice fire safety." (Too generic. If you're going to mention fire safety, be specific.)

### Safety Note (optional)
- Include only when there's a genuine camping-specific safety concern.
- Fire safety, food temperature, bear country considerations, allergy alerts.
- Conditional language: "when cooking over open flame", "if you're in bear country"
- Not every recipe needs one. Only add when it genuinely matters.

**Good:** "Keep raw chicken separate from other food in your cooler. Wash hands and cutting board with camp soap after handling."
**Bad:** "Always exercise caution when cooking." (Too generic. Says nothing useful.)

## Formatting Rules

- No emoji in recipe content or UI copy
- No exclamation marks in recipe content (OK sparingly in UI, e.g., onboarding)
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use commas or periods.
- No "you" in recipe steps. Imperative voice: "Add onions" not "You'll add the onions"
- No food blogger filler words: "simply", "just", "beautiful", "amazing", "perfect"
- Contractions are fine and preferred: "don't" over "do not"
- Sentence case everywhere. No Title Case except recipe names and section headers.
- Numbers under 10 spelled out in prose, numeral in ingredients and times: "Cook for 5 minutes" but "one clove of garlic" in a tip.

## Recipe Difficulty Definitions

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Easy** | One pot/pan, under 30 min total, minimal prep, beginner-friendly |
| **Medium** | May need multiple steps or pans, 30-60 min, some technique helpful |
| **Advanced** | Requires specific skills (fire management, Dutch oven temp control), 60+ min, or multi-stage prep |

Most recipes should be Easy or Medium. Advanced is reserved for things like whole Dutch oven roasts or campfire-baked bread.
